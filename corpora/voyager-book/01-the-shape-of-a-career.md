# Chapter One: The Shape of a Career

A career is not a ladder and it is not measured in years served. It
is a rhythm with two kinds of time, and a history that decides how
they meet.

## Seasons

Seasons are the long quiet. A season is spent one of three ways: in
preparation, deliberately building a capability; in pursuit,
following an interest wherever it goes, useful or not; or in quiet,
holding a life together, which is its own work. Seasons change a
person slowly and privately. Nothing in a season is wasted, but a
season shows nothing to anyone. And a season is an intention, not a
guarantee: the world does not wait for it to finish.

## Moments

Moments are what breaks a season, and they should. A moment is a
situation that demands action or decision now, and it is the only
place a character is revealed. Dice are thrown in moments and only
in moments. What you did with your seasons decides what you bring to
a moment; the moment decides what you are.

Moments come in kinds: a firefight is not a contract, a contract is
not a first contact, and surviving one teaches you nothing about
seeing the next.

## Stage

Stage is the shape of what you have lived. Not a rank, not a total.
Each kind of moment lived sharpens the eye for that kind and that
kind only. A veteran of violence reads an ambush forming across a
street and steps out of it before it closes; the same person signs
the contract built to swallow them whole, blind as any recruit.
Nobody is a veteran of everything.

Seasons raise what you bring to a moment. Moments raise what you
see coming. Whether trouble arrives as fate already resolved or as
doors still open depends on stage, and on nothing kinder: you see
what you have lived, and what you have never lived simply happens
to you.

## A career is a tilt

Choosing a career is choosing your weather: the mercenary's life
sends violence before anything else, the researcher's sends hard
judgment and the unknown. And your history walks into every moment
with you. The doors offered are drawn from what you have lived, so
the veteran of violence finds a violent door offered in every kind
of trouble, whether it belongs there or not. What you practice is
what you become, and what you become is what you are offered. Where
a life diverges from its career's weather, that is where the
stories are.

## The kinds of moments

There are five kinds of moments, and this list is the canon until
this book says otherwise. Each kind is both a thing life throws and
an eye a life can train.

**Violence.** Bodily danger, given or received. The ambush, the
boarding action, the bar that goes quiet. Violence is fast, and its
veterans are people who have learned how much of it is decided
before it starts.

**Judgment.** The hard call under stakes. The contract with the
buried clause, the diagnosis nobody wants to be wrong about, the
trap built out of reasoning. Judgment is slow until it is not, and
its veterans read documents the way soldiers read streets.

**Discovery.** The unknown, the alien, the door off the map. What
answers when nobody expected an answer. Discovery favours the ones
still looking, which is sometimes the well-equipped expedition and
sometimes the one who is out of options and paying attention.

**Standing.** Other people, in numbers or with power. Loyalty and
betrayal, reputation made and spent, the room turning against you.
Standing is the kind of trouble that never announces itself as
trouble, and its veterans hear the announcement anyway.

**Loss.** The kind nobody trains for on purpose. Grief, ruin, the
long way down, the moment where endurance is the only door offered.
Loss reveals more than any other kind, because nothing about it can
be practiced.

## How a moment resolves

At any stage, a moment arrives one of two ways. If your stage in its
kind is beneath it, it arrives as fate: already resolved, the dice
thrown before you saw the table, and you learn what it did to you.
If your eye is good enough, it arrives open: doors are offered, each
stating what it risks and what it reaches for, and the dice wait for
your hand.

And beside every door there is the open one: say what you do, and
the world will price it. What you say becomes a throw the rules
already understand, with its own risk and its own cost, and the
engine rolls it like any other. The world prices; it never gifts.

## Unsettled

Named here so the machinery raises them instead of guessing:

- Whether one moment can carry two kinds at once, a betrayal inside
  a firefight, or whether the fiercest kind claims it whole.
- Whether the open door can drag a moment from one kind into
  another, the scientist turning a firefight into a judgment
  problem.
