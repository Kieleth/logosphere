# Chapter Four: The Director's Playbook

## Whose events these are

Events belong to the world, not to the character. A mining station sends the trouble a mining station sends; a merchant's route sends its own. The director never builds a situation to test this person, teach them, or fit them. What makes an event theirs is only that they were standing there. Related by place and trade and history, never tailored.

## How events arrive

Each season, for each kind, the director states the chance that kind finds this life this season, given its trade, its place, and how the season is being spent, and states it as a probability, never below 0.01 and never above 0.9. The engine draws. What lands, the director dresses. Events may land while another is still open, and when they do the open moment carries both kinds; the fiercest sets the weight.

## The doors

Almost every event offers four ways through it, and the director writes the first three. The direct door meets the event head on. The sideways door meets it through what the character knows, who they know, and what they carry: their trade, their standing with someone present, a mark that matters here, or a detail the event itself has shown. The pass door lets the event happen without them, and the world resolves it as it would have anyway, which is cheaper and blinder than either of the others. The open door is the player's: tell me what you would do, and the director prices it.

Every door states its chance and the two lists, what it risks and what it reaches for. The reward for paying attention is written into the prices: a sideways door or a plan that uses the record and the event's own details is priced better than one that ignores them, and never worse. The world prices; it never gifts.

## What stage shows

Every event offers its doors. A kind the character has never faced shows the doors without their prices; a kind they have faced shows them priced. The eye a life has trained is the difference between choosing and guessing.

## When the making ends

The making of a character ends when the player, at the turn of a season, says it does, or when a turn ends the life.

## The world thickens

Every person, place, and faction the director names becomes part of the world from that moment, held in the record at the scope of this playing, with its standing toward the character. Later trouble may come from them. Which of them enter the book is a decision the book's authors make, never the director.

## The kinds, by example

For Violence, at nothing: a bar goes quiet and stays quiet. At a mark: a broken hand in a loading-bay fight, and a name the dock crews remember. At a turn: a boarding action that ends with a shipmate dead and a debt to the family. At a branch: the ambush that ends the contract, the career, and very nearly the life.

Its doors, in the ambush: direct, hold the line and shoot back. Sideways, the veteran who read the ground uses the spotter's radio traffic the event itself just revealed to slip the squad out under the ridge's blind side. Pass, keep your head down and let the squad lead decide.

## Unsettled

Named here so the machinery raises them instead of guessing:

- The playbook examples for Judgment, Discovery, Standing, and Loss are not written.
- Whether the pass door is always the cheapest, or whether some events punish the bystander most.
