# Chapter Three: What a Life Carries

A character is what the dice made them, the trade they entered, and the record of what they have lived. From this chapter on, a life also carries people and places it has met, each with a standing toward the character; marks, which are lasting changes with a name; and turns, which are the points where the shape of the life changed. What a character can do beyond what they are is written in the chapter that gives them capabilities, and that chapter is not written yet.

## Standings

A standing is what someone or somewhere is to this character: ally, enemy, debt, obligation, or acquaintance. A person, a place, or a faction may hold one standing toward a character at a time, and a later moment may change it.

## What a moment may do

When a moment resolves, it may do nothing, and most do: the record is the whole of it. Otherwise it does one or more of exactly these things, and nothing else is available to a moment.

It may move a characteristic, up or down, by 1 and never more in one moment.

It may bind a standing: a person, a place, or a faction becomes ally, enemy, debt, obligation, or acquaintance to the character.

It may leave a mark: a named lasting fact, a scar, a reputation, a thing known about them where they are known.

It may turn the life, and a turn is one of these: the career ends, the career changes, a forced leaving, or the life ends.

A telling that claims a moment did anything else is a telling and not a rule.

## How hard a moment hits

Every moment has a weight, stated by the director when it arrives and recorded, and a weight is one of these: nothing, a mark, a turn, or a branch. At nothing, a moment may only leave its record. At a mark, it may do one thing from the list and may not turn the life. At a turn, it may do up to three things and may turn the life once. At a branch, it may turn the life and mark it as it likes, and branches are rare because the director's own rates make them rare, not because a rule says so.

## How effects combine

All the effects of one resolution land together or none of them do. Two effects that pull the same characteristic in opposite directions in one moment are refused, not netted. A turn that ends the career or the life ends the season with it, and no further event lands in that season. What a door reaches for and what it risks are both written in these effects, so a player choosing a door is choosing between two lists, not between two adjectives.

## Unsettled

Named here so the machinery raises them instead of guessing:

- Whether a life may end during its making, or whether the last turn on the list waits until there is a game around the character to lose.
- Whether a mark can be shed, and what it costs.
