# Voyager — the universe this game is played in

_DRAFT, for the owner to cut and rewrite. Cepheus is an engine and says
so: "A Referee may use a published setting for their adventures, or
create their own." This file is our setting. Nothing here contradicts
the SRD; it gives the rules a place to happen._

_The narrator reads this file and nothing else about the setting. If a
thing is not written here, the narrator does not know it._

## The shape of it

The jump drive works and nobody can explain why it works the way it
does. Ships cross a parsec in a week of subjective quiet and arrive
where they meant to, mostly. The routes that hold are the ones people
have run enough times to trust, and the map is really a map of habits.

There is no empire. There was something once, far enough back that the
word for it has drifted, and what remains is the wreckage of its
logistics: relay stations still broadcasting on channels nobody
answers, orbital yards half-cannibalised into homes, standards that
everyone still obeys because the alternative is not fitting anyone
else's docking clamp. Credits are honoured everywhere for exactly this
reason. Nobody issues them. Everybody takes them.

Authority is local and thin. A world's law level is the honest measure
of how much of it there is. High-law worlds are old, comfortable and
watchful. Low-law worlds are where the work is.

## Psionics

Some people can do things with their minds that instruments record and
no one can account for. It is not common, it is not mystical, and it is
not welcome.

The talent runs in families and skips generations. It surfaces under
strain more often than in comfort, which means the frontier finds more
psionics than the core does, which the core takes as evidence of what
the frontier is. High-law worlds test for it. Some register it. A few
prohibit it outright, in the flat administrative language reserved for
things that frighten people.

A character's psionic strength is not rolled at creation. It is found
out, usually badly, usually later. Nobody says the word casually.

## Out there

**The Reach** is the frontier: worlds settled within living memory or
being settled now, thin populations, good air on maybe one in five.
Everything is scarce except distance.

**The Old Lanes** are the routes the vanished logistics left behind.
They are safe in the sense that they are known. They pass through
places that were important to somebody once.

**Belters** work rock and ice at the edges of systems and consider
planetary life a form of dependency. **Scouts** map the Reach for
whoever is paying, and come back changed or not at all. **Nobles** hold
titles that meant something under an authority that no longer exists,
which makes their standing a social fact rather than a legal one.
**Drifters** are what the frontier calls you when nothing else will
have you, and there are more of them than anyone counts.

## Tone

Working people in a big quiet dark. Competence is respected, ceremony
is not. Distances are real: a passage costs money and weeks, and people
say goodbye properly because they mean it.

Not heroic. Not grim. Things break, people fix them, and sometimes the
fix holds.
